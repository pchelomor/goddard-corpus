Hard editorial pass completed under `EDITORIAL_POLICY.md` using `SOURCE_FIRST_CANONICAL` mode.

## Why source-first mode was required
The worker retained **3,036 candidate windows** from a canonical source of **798 paragraphs**, with **357 duplicate groups**. Candidate-by-candidate review would therefore spend most of the pass on overlapping windows and repeated testimonial material.

## Main editorial actions
- Preserved the strongest Neville-only formulations on imagination, thinking from the end, sensory vividness, revision, attention, moods, attitudes, and deliberate participation in an imaginal scene.
- Treated the many letters and case histories as evidence/context, not as Neville-authored quotation text. Language belonging to correspondents was not moved into the curated Neville pool.
- Excluded pure scripture and external wording from Blake, Shakespeare, Yeats, Herbert, Shelley, Rossetti, Fawcett, and other quoted authors.
- Collapsed repeated `imagining creates reality`, `thinking of / thinking from`, wish-fulfilled, revision, and attention formulations to the cleanest local versions.
- Kept gambling/race-track winnings, categorical financial outcomes, and similar material out of the automatic pool.
- Excluded medical cure stories and passages that could imply that imagination should replace medical treatment or that illness is simply caused by mental activity.
- Excluded or held claims assigning imaginal causation to accidents, the Titanic disaster, war, deaths, other people's behavior, legal verdicts, or other harmful/context-sensitive events.
- Held bilocation, remote-presence, direct mental influence, and strong sensory-evidence-denial passages for contextual study rather than automatic delivery.
- Treated Chapter 15, **The Promise: Four Mystical Experiences**, as a spiritual/Promise opt-in layer. Its supernatural birth, Davidic fatherhood, Golgotha/skull, serpent/light, and related mystical testimony was not converted into context-free main-pool maxims.

## Strongest main-pool clusters
1. **Imagination as deliberate practice** — entering the image and acting from it rather than merely observing it.
2. **Thinking from the end** — occupying the fulfilled state instead of thinking about it from outside.
3. **Revision** — replaying remembered scenes with new content and making revision a regular practice.
4. **Attention and mood** — directing attention and deliberately cultivating the mood associated with the desired state.
5. **Creative change rather than resistance** — changing the imaginal pattern instead of trying to force or control people and events.
