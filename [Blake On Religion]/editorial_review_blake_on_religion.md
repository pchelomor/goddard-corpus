## Editorial result

This lecture required unusually strict attribution handling because Neville quotes and discusses William Blake extensively. Direct Blake passages, Blake's aphorisms, and other external wording are not eligible as Neville quotations even when they are memorable.

Two Neville-only formulations survive hard selection: a clean analogy separating the occupant from the state, and a compact statement about remaining loyal to an imaginal act. Much of the remaining Neville commentary is useful only with Blake/religious context or carries strong mystical, postmortem, universal-causation, or control-of-others claims.

The worker layer was used only for discovery/provenance. Full coverage comes from reading all 115 canonical paragraphs end-to-end.
